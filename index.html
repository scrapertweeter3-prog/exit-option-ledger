Leaving is part of the price

A $5,995 monthly subscription that cancels anytime has a clean worst case: one month. Sign on the first, walk on the thirtieth, and the entire downside is $5,995, with a private GitHub repo of approved work still sitting in your account. Almost nothing else in custom software pricing makes the downside that small.

Compare the shapes the industry usually sells. A 50 percent deposit before line one ships is common, and on a $60,000 engagement that is $30,000 committed to a delivery date you do not control. A six-month minimum is another standard shape; at this subscription's price that would be $35,970 of spend locked in before an exit exists at all.

Keep-your-code only means something because of how the code arrives. Work lands in a private GitHub repo as each card is approved, not in a handover archive after a final invoice. Six weeks in, the repo already holds everything that shipped, so canceling is a non-event: next month's $5,995 never bills and the asset does not move.

The option has a list price too. Monthly runs $5,995 against $4,995 on the yearly rung, so the right to leave any month costs $1,000 a month, $12,000 a year. Carry monthly while the first cards prove the loop, 72-hour turnaround on one active request at a time through Trello, then take the yearly price once walking away stops being plausible.
